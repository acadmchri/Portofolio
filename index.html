BUSINESS ANALYSIS IN THE AGE OF AI

Better operations start
with better understanding.

Business analysis helps organizations understand what needs to improve. AI can help them act on that understanding faster—but the business still needs people to guide the change.

Business analysis is important because operational problems are rarely isolated. Delays, duplicated work, poor communication, inaccurate forecasts, and disconnected systems can affect several departments at once. A Business Analyst helps identify the root problem, understand the people and processes involved, and translate business needs into clear requirements and practical improvements. The goal is not simply to introduce new technology; it is to create measurable business value.
01 / UNDERSTAND

Find the real problem

Map existing workflows, gather stakeholder input, examine performance data, and identify bottlenecks or unnecessary steps before recommending a solution.

02 / IMPROVE

Make operations more effective

Clarify responsibilities, reduce duplicated work, improve handoffs between teams, and define requirements for processes or systems that support consistent execution.

03 / MEASURE

Connect change to outcomes

Define useful measures—such as processing time, error rates, operating costs, customer response time, or forecast accuracy—to evaluate whether an improvement is working.

WHERE AI ADDS VALUE

AI can accelerate the work.
Business judgment guides it.

AI is most useful when it is applied to a clearly understood business need, supported by reliable data, and reviewed by people who understand the organization.

01 / AUTOMATION

Reduce repetitive work

AI can assist with summarizing meetings, sorting requests, drafting reports, extracting information, and routing routine tasks—giving employees more time for work that needs human attention.

02 / INSIGHT

Spot patterns sooner

AI-supported analysis can help examine large datasets, identify unusual activity, detect customer trends, and support forecasting. Analysts then validate what the patterns mean in the business context.

03 / COORDINATION

Connect people and systems

Well-designed AI workflows can help information move between functions such as sales, operations, finance, and customer service, reducing delays caused by disconnected processes.

Example: connecting a business workflow

Sales data can inform demand forecasts; forecasts can help operations plan inventory; inventory needs can inform purchasing and finance; and customer trends can help marketing plan campaigns. A Business Analyst helps define the information, rules, handoffs, and responsibilities across this workflow. AI may assist with forecasting, alerts, and routine coordination, while people review exceptions and make accountable decisions.

Why the Business Analyst remains important: AI can generate summaries, suggest patterns, and draft requirements, but it may miss context, misunderstand stakeholder needs, or produce inaccurate recommendations. Business Analysts help frame the right problem, check the quality and relevance of AI outputs, consider risks and user impact, and ensure that a proposed solution supports the organization's actual objectives. In other words, AI can support the analysis, but people remain responsible for judgment, alignment, and outcomes.

Further reading: How AI is Reshaping the Role of Business Analyst — Sparkling Logic. The article discusses AI as a productivity tool and the evolving BA role in designing AI workflows and establishing appropriate guidelines and human oversight.
